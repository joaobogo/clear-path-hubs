# TaaSFlow Tone of Voice — Confidence Without Ego

Purpose: make TaaSFlow read as the obvious choice through **evidence, transparency,
specificity, comparisons, recruiter credibility, product proof, and math** —
never through superlatives.

If a sentence could appear on any other recruiting site, rewrite it.

---

## 1. Principles

1. **Evidence over adjective.** State what happens, in numbers, not how great it is.
2. **Transparency over polish.** Name the tradeoff before the reader has to ask.
3. **Specificity over scope.** "Series A–C operators hiring 5–20 roles a quarter"
   beats "growing companies."
4. **Comparison over claim.** "Flat $5,900/mo vs. 22% of first-year salary on a
   $150k hire" beats "cheaper than agencies."
5. **Recruiter credibility.** Written by people who have run searches, not by a
   marketing team describing recruiting from the outside.
6. **Product proof.** Screens, ranked lists, evidence rows — the workspace is the
   demo. The site shows it, not describes it.
7. **Math shows up.** Rates, throughput, coverage %, time-to-shortlist, retention.

---

## 2. Ban list — never ship these

Reject on sight, even in drafts:

- best-in-class · world-leading · world-class
- revolutionary · disruptive · game-changing · game-changer
- cutting-edge AI · next-generation · state-of-the-art
- unmatched · unrivaled · unparalleled · industry-leading
- AI-powered platform · powered by AI · magically · seamlessly
- 10x · effortless · frictionless · turnkey
- "trusted by leading brands" (without a named list)
- "revolutionizing how [X] hires"
- empty verbs: leverage, unlock, empower, transform, elevate, supercharge
- vague scope: "growing companies", "modern teams", "businesses of all sizes"

If a claim requires one of these words to survive, the claim is not proven yet.

## 4. Confidence-without-ego copy system

Four moves. Any commercial paragraph should use at least two.

### Move A — Anchor in a number
Replace adjectives with a number the reader can verify or estimate.
> Not: "A powerful workspace." → "A workspace 42 clients logged into last week."

### Move B — Name the tradeoff
Say what the product is not, before the reader guesses.
> "We are not an ATS. We do not replace your HRIS. We deliver the shortlist and
> the evidence behind it."

### Move C — Compare to the alternative
Force the reader to hold two options in mind — subscription vs. contingency, or
in-house recruiter vs. TaaSFlow.
> "One senior hire at $150k costs $33,000 in contingency fees. A TaaSFlow
> Growth month is $5,900."

### Move D — Show the artifact
End with the artifact — the shortlist, the evidence row, the pipeline card.
Don't describe it. Link to it or render it.

## 6. House rules

- Say "recruiter" not "talent partner."
- Say "shortlist" not "candidate pool."
- Say "flat subscription" not "predictable pricing."
- Say "evidence" not "insights."
- Numbers are digits (5, not five) when they carry a claim.
- Cite the source when quoting a number ("22% contingency fee — market rate for
  senior tech roles, Ph. Creelman 2024" style parentheticals are fine).
- One claim per sentence. No stacked adjectives.
- If you can delete an adjective and the sentence still means the same thing,
  delete it.

## 7. Review checklist (before shipping any hero, section, or CTA)

- [ ] Zero banned phrases.
- [ ] At least one number, comparison, or named artifact per paragraph.
- [ ] The reader can explain the product after 5 seconds.
- [ ] The claim would still be true if a competitor's logo replaced ours? → rewrite.
- [ ] The tradeoff is named somewhere on the page.
- [ ] The artifact is shown (screen, ranked row, evidence line), not described.
